Panels, I-Joists, SIPs & Joinery

7.1

Engineered framing: Glulam & LVL

Glulam (glue-laminated timber): select lumber laminations, end-joined with finger or scarf joints, glued with adhesives chosen for expected moisture.

Why laminate? To create sizes and shapes not available naturally — huge beams, arches, curves (churches, lodges) — and to improve quality by removing/controlling defects and grain direction.

LVL (laminated veneer lumber): thin veneers glued with grain all parallel. Used for beams, headers, and I-joist flanges.

7.2

Plywood & how veneers are graded

  • Thin veneers (rotary-cut — peeled off a spinning log) glued together
  • Odd number of veneers, alternating direction, face veneers parallel
  • 4' × 8' panels, ¼" to 1" thick
  • Uses like OSB (sheathing), plus exposed/painted surfaces and concrete formwork; costs more than OSB
Why cross the grain?

Wood barely moves along its grain but moves a lot across it. Crossing layers locks each other in place → stable, strong both directions.

Softwood plywood veneer grades

On the exam

What determines a softwood plywood veneer grade? The size and number of allowable defects (such as knots and splits). Not span rating, width, or finish coats.

7.3

Non-veneered panels: OSB, particleboard, fiberboard

On the exam

Two common structural wood sheathing products: plywood and OSB.

7.4

Reading an APA panel stamp

The APA rates panels used for sheathing and siding on structural adequacy, dimensional stability, and durability of the adhesive.

  • Span rating (e.g. 24/16): bigger number = roof rafter spacing, smaller = floor joist spacing, in inches
  • Exposure durability: Exterior = high-quality veneers + waterproof glue, for continuous exposure (siding). Exposure 1 = waterproof glue, lower-quality veneers, for short-term exposure during construction (subfloors, sheathing) — the most common rating.
  • Panels are undersized ⅛" to allow spacing between them.
7.5

Wood trusses

  • Mostly factory-built (pre-engineered) from 2×4 or 2×6 lumber joined by toothed plate fasteners
  • Members form an array of interconnected triangles
  • Stock or special order, many shapes: parallel-chord trusses for floors & flat roofs; sloping top-chord trusses for pitched roofs
Why trusses or I-joists instead of 2× lumber?

More efficient use of wood · span farther / carry more · more dimensionally stable · faster to frame (often 24" o.c. or more).

7.6

Wood I-joists

  • I-shaped: flanges of solid wood or LVL, web of OSB or plywood
  • Structurally efficient, dimensionally stable, light; you can cut holes in the web for ducts/pipes
  • Typically 9½"–24" deep, up to 40 ft+ long; cost comparable to 2× lumber
  • Proprietary — each manufacturer designs its own and publishes structural data, span tables, and connection details
On the exam

Common I-joist web material = OSB. Verify allowable span in the manufacturer's span tables (for that specific series, depth, and loading).

Quick check
Which option pairs a common wood I-joist web material with the source for its allowable span?
OSB web + manufacturer's span tables.
7.7

Structural Insulated Panels (SIPs)

A sandwich panel: two outer sheets of OSB glued to a core of rigid foam plastic insulation. Used in homes and commercial buildings — often to fill in the walls and roof of a heavy timber frame, fastened with special long screws.

Think of it as an I-beam

OSB skins = flanges, foam = web. Bonded together, the foam keeps the skins working as a team (transfers shear) while insulating.

On the exam

A SIP has an insulating foam core (not hollow, not solid lumber, not gypsum).

7.8

Joinery: dovetail, mortise & tenon, steel plates

Heavy timber connections are called joinery.

  • Traditional: mortise-and-tenon, dovetail (old-fashioned look), often pinned with wood pegs
  • Modern: steel plates with bolts, nuts & washers (tops of posts squared off to make the connection easier)
On the exam

A dovetail's flared, tapered shape interlocks with a matching recess and resists straight pullout.

Quick check
Which pair correctly describes a structural insulated panel and a dovetail joint?
Foam core + interlocking tapered shapes.
