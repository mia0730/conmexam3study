Cavity Walls, Flashing, Weeps & Joints

The big idea: brick leaks — on purpose, that's fine. A cavity wall expects water to get through the brick, catches it in the air space, and sends it back outside with flashing + weeps. Picture the water's path and these questions answer themselves.

4.1

Anatomy of a brick-veneer cavity wall (at the base)

A cavity wall (also called a drainage wall) = exterior cladding (skin) + inner backup wall + an air space between. With masonry, the skin is usually brick or stone veneer. Very common in commercial construction today.

Practice Q: list the layers, outside → in

Brick veneer → clear drainage cavity → rigid foam insulation → CMU backup.
At the base, the veneer bears on a ledge of the concrete foundation, which also supports the CMU backup.

How it works: moisture that gets through the brick collects in the cavity, runs down the back of the brick to the bottom, where flashing catches it and weep holes let it out.

Memory hook: "Catch it, Carry it, Kick it out"

Cavity catches the water → flashing carries it → weeps kick it out.

Rigid insulation can sit in the cavity (some has mortar-catching netting at the bottom so mortar droppings don't clog the weeps). Keep the drainage space clear.

4.2

The #1 exam picture: water above a window lintel

  1. Water runs down the cavity (the back of the brick).
  2. It lands on the head flashing above the lintel, which is turned up the backup wall so water can't get inside.
  3. The flashing slopes/leads it outward …
  4. … and it exits through weeps immediately above the flashing.
On the exam

"It should run down the cavity to flashing and exit through weeps above the lintel." ✔
Not: soak into the lintel ✘ · flow through the backup to an interior sill ✘ · pass through insulation to the foundation ✘.

Sill flashing (below a window) catches leaks from the bottom of the window; its ends are turned up to form end dams and it extends beyond the exterior face.

Quick check
How should water that reaches the cavity above a window lintel drain?
Cavity → flashing → weeps. Water never goes inward.
4.3

Masonry ties — connecting veneer to backup

Ties hold the veneer back against lateral loads (wind pushing/pulling). They are not what holds up the brick's weight — the veneer bears on the foundation or a shelf angle.

Tie typeNotes
Adjustable (eye & pintle)Ideal when veneer & backup coursing don't match
CorrugatedWavy metal strip; nailed to a wood frame backup, or set in a CMU joint
3-wire truss tieReinforces the CMU joint and ties brick to CMU

Practice Q7

An adjustable tie anchors the veneer to the backup for lateral support and adjusts to align with the veneer mortar joint. A backup wall other than CMU: a sheathed wood-stud wall (metal studs work too).

Quick check
What is the primary purpose of an adjustable tie between brick veneer and a backup wall?
Lateral support + adjustability. Ties should never carry water inward and don't hold the veneer's weight.
4.4

Flashing & weep holes — the rules

Flashing

Prevents water passing through a joint in a wall, roof, or chimney, and directs the flow of water. Made of thin metal (copper, stainless, galvanized steel), plastic, rubber, or waterproof paper.

Weep holes

  • Work with flashing to let moisture drain out
  • Made by leaving mortar out of part of a head joint
  • Spaced 16–24 in apart horizontally (every 2–3 modular brick)
  • Rope wicks or plastic inserts can keep the joint open

4.5

Shelf angles & horizontal joints

Shelf angles are steel angles attached to the building frame or backup wall, horizontally every one or two stories. They support the brick veneer above them.

A space is left under each shelf angle so the brick below can expand vertically — that's a horizontal (movement) joint, filled with flexible backer rod + caulking to seal out air and water. Flashing & weeps sit on top of the angle.

4.6

Expansion & control joints — why and where

Why joints? Masonry moves with temperature and moisture. A long wall with no joints builds up stress and cracks. Two walls meeting at a corner (say a north and west wall) move different amounts.

BrickCMU
Moves by…Expanding — absorbs moisture after kiln; sun heatShrinking — steam-cured moist, dries out
Joint nameExpansion jointControl joint
Built asA gap through the wythe + backer rod + sealant. (In the field the names get swapped — built the same way.)
Control joints

Break a CMU wall into segments so as the block dries and shrinks, the wall contracts at the joint instead of cracking randomly.

Quick check
What is a purpose of vertical expansion joints in fired-clay brick veneer?
Bone-dry fired brick soaks up moisture and grows. The joint compresses so the wall doesn't crack.
4.7

Prefabricated masonry & the sandwich panel

Prefabricated masonry panels are built indoors under controlled conditions, shipped to the site, and attached to the frame — used like precast concrete panels as a decorative skin. Reinforcing runs vertically and horizontally through the panel and is grouted in.

On the exam — 3 layers, in order

Masonry (or concrete) — rigid insulation — masonry (or concrete). Two hard faces with foam in the middle. The MC version calls it an insulated precast concrete sandwich panel: concrete, insulation, concrete.

Hook

It's literally a sandwich: bread – filling – bread. The foam is the filling, never the bread.

Quick check
Which sequence shows the main layers of an insulated precast concrete sandwich panel?
Hard – foam – hard.
