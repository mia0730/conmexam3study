1.1

Masonry 101 — what we're building with

Masonry = building with small units stacked in mortar. Materials include concrete products (concrete block = CMU, concrete brick, pavers), clay products (brick, quarry & ceramic tile), and stone & glass (stone veneer, glass block).

This unit focuses on two: Concrete Masonry Units (CMUs) and clay brick.

How brick is used today:

  • Veneer (facing) — a thin outer skin of brick with a backup wall behind it for support. Backup can be wood studs, metal studs, or CMU.
  • Solid brick walls — often decorative now.
  • Flooring & paving.
Why people love brick

Small units → intricate patterns, arches, mosaics. Mortar joints can be colored to contrast or blend with the brick.

1.2

Course vs. wythe (and head vs. bed joints)

Course
A horizontal row of masonry units.
Wythe
A continuous vertical layer of masonry one unit thick.
Bed joint
The horizontal mortar joint the unit sits ("lies in bed") on.
Head joint
The vertical mortar joint between the ends of units.
Memory hook

Course = Cross the wall (side to side). Wythe = how many Walls thick (front to back). A 2-wythe wall is two bricks thick.

On the exam

"A course is a horizontal row of units, and a wythe is a continuous layer one unit thick." — that exact pairing is the right answer.

Quick check
A wall is described as "two wythes." What does that tell you?
Wythe = vertical layer one unit thick. Two wythes = two layers = two bricks thick.
1.3

The six brick positions

Same brick, six ways to set it. What matters is which face shows on the wall and whether the brick is lying flat, on edge, or standing up. The bright red face is the one you see from outside.

Exam focus: stretcher · header · soldier

Stretcher — lies flat, long face exposed horizontally. (The "normal" brick.)
Header — lies flat, short end exposed. It reaches back into the wall, so headers can tie two wythes together.
Soldier — stands upright on end, long narrow face exposed. Often a row over windows.

Memory hooks

A stretcher stretches out lengthwise. A header shows you its head (the end). A soldier stands tall and skinny at attention. A sailor stands up but puffs out his broad chest. A rowlock is a header rolled onto its edge. A shiner shows off its big broad face, sideways.

Quick check
Which brick orientation presents the long face of the brick horizontally?
Stretcher = lying flat, long face showing horizontally. Soldier shows the long face too — but vertically.
1.4

Bond patterns

Pattern bond = the arrangement of units in the wall face. Patterns are used for looks, and (before metal ties and joint reinforcement existed) to overlap bricks so the wall acts as one structural whole. Headers are drawn darker so you can spot them.

BondRecognize it by…
Runningall stretchers, joints offset ½ brick
Common / Americanrunning bond + a header course every ~6th course
FlemishH‑S‑H‑S alternating within every course
Englishwhole courses alternate: all S, then all H
Stackjoints line up vertically

CMU walls usually use running or stack bond.

Classic trap

Flemish = alternate within each course. English = alternate whole courses. The quiz literally offers "a full course of headers alternating with a full course of stretchers" — that's English, not Flemish.

Quick check
Which arrangement identifies Flemish bond?
Flemish = H, S, H, S across every single course. (Option C is English bond; D is stack bond.)
1.5

How clay brick is made

Brick types

Solid, cored (holes through), hollow, and frogged (a recess in one bed face).

Standard vs. modular brick

WidthHeightLength
Standard3⅝"2¼"8"
Modular3⅝"2¼"7⅝"

Modular is ⅜" short so that brick + ⅜" mortar joint = an even 8" — it lines up with CMU (see Lesson 5).

Practice-quiz answer, in your words

"Clay or shale is prepared, shaped, dried, and fired in a kiln." That's the whole story in one sentence — memorize it.

1.6

Why brick grows after firing (and block shrinks)

Brick leaves the kiln with essentially zero moisture. Over time it absorbs moisture from the environment and expands. Dark brick also heats up in the sun and expands.

A vertical expansion joint is a planned gap through the brick veneer, filled with a squishy backer rod + sealant. As the brick grows sideways, the joint compresses instead of the wall cracking.

Memory hook

Brick = Bigger → Expansion joint. CMU (concrete) = Contracts → Control joint.

On the exam

Purpose of vertical expansion joints in fired-clay brick veneer = to accommodate expansion as the fired brick absorbs moisture. They do not carry weight, drain water, or add bond.
