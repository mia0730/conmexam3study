8.2

Platform frame (vs. old balloon frame)

The platform frame is built one floor at a time: floor platform → one-story walls → next platform → next walls.

  • Each stud runs one story → short, easy-to-handle studs
  • Each platform is a work surface for the next floor → safer
  • Firestops are automatic — the platform blocks the wall cavity at each floor
  • Building height isn't limited by available stud length
On the exam

In platform framing, wall studs are typically one story tall. (Two stories / foundation-to-roof = balloon framing.)

8.3

Floor framing: which way does everything run?

  • Parallel, closely spaced joists with a rim joist around the edge
  • Joists: 2×8, 2×10, 2×12, I-joists, or trusses at 12, 16, or 24" o.c.
  • Joists span the shortest dimension between supports (shorter span = smaller joists)
  • Supported by foundation walls, load-bearing stud walls, or carrying beams
  • Framing is strengthened at openings and overhangs
On the exam (Q21)

Joists run across the shorter span, and the sheathing strength axis runs perpendicular to them.

Hook

"Short span, cross the plank." Joists go the short way; plywood/OSB goes across the joists like a bridge deck across beams.

Floor sheathing install: plywood or OSB, long dimension perpendicular to joists, nailed or screwed, glued to stop squeaks, end joints staggered.

Bridging & blocking (wood or metal) give lateral restraint: improve rigidity, hold joists straight, and distribute loads. (Lecture: code requires it for joists deeper than 2×12.)

Joist hangers provide anchorage and support where a joist frames into a beam or header.

A gable roof has two sloping planes that meet at a ridge, forming triangular end walls (gables). Four slopes meeting at hips = a hip roof (wrong answer).

8.5

Three ways to frame a roof

Rafter & ridge board assembly

  • Rafters rest on the exterior walls and meet the opposite rafter at the ridge
  • Rafters: 2×6, 2×8, or 2×10 at 12, 16, or 24" o.c.
  • Ceiling joists connect the rafters at the walls → they tie the walls together against the rafters' outward thrust (tension)
  • Collar ties between rafters counter uplift; ridge straps also help

Rafter & ridge beam

  • Rafters rest on walls at the bottom and on a structural beam at the ridge
  • The ridge beam supports the upper ends of the rafters and carries their load down to posts or other supports
  • No ceiling joists needed → used for vaulted / cathedral ceilings
On the exam (Q22)

Gable roof with a structural ridge beam: two slopes meet at the ridge, and the beam supports the upper ends of the rafters.

Ridge BOARD vs ridge BEAM

A ridge board is just a nailing surface where rafters lean on each other — the ceiling joists do the work. A ridge beam is structural and holds the rafters up.

8.6

The bird's-mouth cut

A bird's mouth is a notch cut in the underside of a rafter where it bears on the exterior wall's top plate. It looks like an open bird's beak biting the plate.

It has a horizontal seat cut (rests flat on the plate) and a vertical plumb cut. A hurricane clip (metal tie) then fastens the rafter/truss to the plate against uplift.

On the exam (Q23)

The bird's-mouth cut in a common rafter bears on the wall's top plate — not the ridge, not the sheathing, not the fascia.

8.7

Sheathing → felt → shingles (and house wrap)

On the exam (Q24)

Roof sheathing goes on BEFORE the roof covering. The sheathing forms the continuous deck that supports the underlayment and the roof covering — shingles need something solid to nail into.

Sheathing (OSB or plywood): joins & stabilizes the structure, resists uplift, resists racking from lateral forces, and provides a surface for finish materials. Metal edge clips (H-clips) support roof panel edges between rafters. Note the OSB strand orientation.

Roofing felt: recycled paper & sawdust impregnated with asphalt; underlayment for the finished roofing; rolls 30" wide × 50 or 100 ft; now sold as #15 or #30 (formerly "15 lb/30 lb"). Not truly "tar paper" (that's virgin pulp + tar).

House wrap (walls): a water barrier that keeps liquid water out but lets water vapor pass, resists air infiltration; made of tiny polyethylene fibers. Then siding.

8.9

Heavy timber construction

  • Skeletal — resembles structural steel: widely spaced timber posts (columns) and beams; posts carry loads to the foundation
  • Roof on timber rafters or trusses; purlins span across them to shorten the span for decking or sandwich panels
  • Tongue & groove decking carries floor and roof loads to the frame
  • Walls are filled in with conventional stud walls or SIPs (fastened with special screws)
  • During construction, bents (a post-and-beam cross frame) are assembled on the ground, stacked, then hoisted by crane
  • Knee braces (short diagonals at post-beam corners) help resist lateral loads
  • Connections = joinery: traditional mortise-and-tenon / dovetail, or modern steel plates & bolts
