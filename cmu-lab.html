"Six-inch wall, three-eighths joint, cave it in." — 6 · ⅜ · concave.

Why these make sense

⅜" is the standard joint for CMU (nominal = actual + ⅜"). Concave is the best water-shedding tooled profile.

5.2

Ladder & truss joint reinforcement

Joint reinforcement is a prefabricated wire grid laid in the mortar to improve joint strength (and control cracking). Usually 9-gauge galvanized steel, sold in 10-ft lengths, in two shapes:

  • Ladder — two side rods with straight cross rungs
  • Truss — two side rods with a zig-zag diagonal
Where does it go?

In the horizontal mortar bed joints — never in the vertical head joints, never on the face of the wall.

The math

Each CMU course = 8 in (with joint). 16 in o.c. ÷ 8 in = 2 → reinforcement in every second course (the reinforced joints are two courses apart).

Quick check
If each CMU course is 8 in high including mortar, how many courses apart is joint reinforcement placed for 16 in on center vertically?
16 ÷ 8 = 2.
Quick check
Where is ladder or truss joint reinforcement placed in a CMU wall?
It lies flat in the bed joint, embedded in mortar.
5.3

Brick ↔ CMU coursing: 3 to 1

3 modular brick courses = 1 CMU course = 8 in

(Including the mortar joints.) A modular brick course is about 2⅔" tall; three of them stack to 8". That's why modular brick and CMU work together in a cavity wall — their bed joints line up every 8" so ties and joint reinforcement can cross the cavity.

Also remember

12 modular bricks (with joints) take up the same volume as 1 CMU.

Quick check
How many standard modular brick courses match the height of one 8-inch CMU course (with joints)?
3 × 2⅔" = 8".
