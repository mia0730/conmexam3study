CMU: Walls, Units & Reinforcing

Concrete block isn't just "gray bricks." Different wall jobs (bearing, partition, shear) and a whole family of special-shaped units — corner, bullnose, bond beam — each with one clear purpose.

2.1

What CMU walls do

Six common uses of CMU walls: foundation walls (instead of cast-in-place concrete), bearing walls, shear walls, interior partitions, backup walls (behind brick veneer), and retaining walls.

Shear walls give stiffness to the building frame — used in both concrete and steel frames, and needed to resist wind in both directions (so architects place them creatively).

On the exam: partition vs. shear wall

A nonbearing CMU partition divides space and carries only its own weight.
A CMU shear wall resists in-plane lateral forces from wind or earthquakes and transfers them to the foundation.

Quick check
Which wall is designed to resist in-plane lateral loads such as those from wind?
"Lateral loads" + "in-plane" = shear wall. A partition just divides space.
2.2

CMU sizes — nominal vs. actual

CMU is a modular system. Nominal (named) size includes the mortar joint; the actual block is ⅜" smaller.

Two modular facts to know cold

3 courses of modular brick (with joints) = 1 CMU course (8").
12 modular bricks (with joints) occupy the same volume as 1 CMU.

Common CMU widths are named by nominal thickness: 4", 6", 8", 10", 12". (Your CMU lab used a 6-inch nominal wall.)

Corner → wall ends / outside corners. Bullnose → rounded edges. Bond beam → horizontal steel. Every wrong option scrambles these three — lock in the pairs.

Which set correctly matches CMU units to their uses?
Each name tells you its job: corner at corners/ends, bull-nose = rounded nose, bond beam = a beam (needs horizontal steel).
2.4

Bond beams & reinforced masonry

Reinforced masonry is usually part of bearing wall construction. For mid-rise buildings, walls need very high compressive and flexural (bending) strength while staying a reasonable thickness → so we grout rebar into the cavities of the block.

Bond beams strengthen a wall where floors and roofs frame into it (look for one directly below where the slab rests). They run the length of the wall and may be more than one course tall.

Grout = cement + sand + water (sometimes an admixture to reduce shrinkage). Fills voids in CMU walls and works with rebar to add strength. Placed by pump or bucket, in low-lift or high-lift pours.

Strength of a masonry wall depends on

① the masonry unit ② the mortar ③ reinforcing & ties ④ grout.

2.5

Spanning openings: lintels & arches

Lintels are short beams over openings. They carry wall loads around the opening to the wall on each side and keep wall loads off the window/door frames.

2.7

Laying masonry & scaffolding

  1. Lay out the wall location on the footing.
  2. Build the leads (stepped corners/ends) first — set precisely level and plumb.
  3. Lay to the line — a string pulled between leads; fill in between them. Joint reinforcement goes into the bed joints as you go.
  4. Tool the joints, then brush off mortar once it's set enough. (Muriatic acid sometimes cleans mortar off brick — not needed for CMU.)

Most masonry is built in place: slow, weather-dependent, and needs scaffolding as a work platform.
