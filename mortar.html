Lesson 3 · Mortar & joints

Mortar: Ingredients, Types & Tooling

The exam question here is really about two ingredients and one action: what sand does, what hydrated lime does, and why joints are tooled. Everything else on this page supports those three.

What mortar doesIngredientsTypes M-S-N-OTooling & profiles
3.1

What mortar does

Mortar functions:

  • Bonds masonry units into a structural whole
  • Provides full bearing despite surface irregularities
  • Seals spaces between units from air & water
  • Adds to aesthetics (color, profile)

Important properties: compressive strength, flexural strength, workability & suction, permeability, color & appearance.

Weak link

Masonry units are typically stronger than the mortar — mortar is the weak link in a wall.

3.2

The four ingredients & their jobs

Lime — the full list from lecture

  • ✔ Increases workability and suction of fresh mortar
  • ✔ Helps fresh mortar retain moisture longer
  • ✔ Promotes a better bond to the units
  • ✔ Improves the water resistance of the hardened wall
  • ✘ Reduces compressive strength

Sand must be clean (no silt/clay), free of organics, properly graded (a range of sizes), and less coarse than concrete sand. Comes from screened/washed embankments or crushed quarry stone.

Other ways to mix: sand + masonry cement + water, or a pre-blended masonry mix (just add water). Admixtures: coloring agents, retarders.

On the exam

Sand provides bulk (and helps limit shrinkage). Hydrated lime improves workability (and water retention). Tooling compacts the joint surface.

Trap answers to reject

"Sand is the main binder" ✘ (cement is). "Lime is the aggregate" ✘ (sand is). "Lime is only a pigment" ✘. "Tooling opens the joint surface" ✘ (it closes/compacts it).

Water & retempering timeline

Retempering = adding water to soften mortar that's started to stiffen. Unlike concrete, mortar wants as much water as possible while still workable.

Quick check
Which statement correctly describes masonry mortar?
Sand = bulk. Lime = workability. Tooling = compacts. This is exactly the instructor quiz question.

Mortar types: M · S · N · O

TypeCharacterUse
MHighest compressive, best vs severe frost; least lime → least workable, poor adhesion, more permeableBelow-ground foundation walls
SHighest flexural strength, high compressiveExterior load-bearing walls in seismic/high-wind areas, chimneys, interior bearing walls
NModerate; high lime → workable, low permeabilityGeneral purpose, non-load-bearing; veneer walls
OLow strength, high limeInterior non-load-bearing only; restoration of old masonry
Golden rule

Specify the weakest mortar that will do the job. Weaker = more lime = better workability and a more watertight wall.

3.4

Tooling & joint profiles

Tooling = running a jointer (or the trowel) along the joint once the mortar has partly stiffened (lecture: usually 2–3 hours after laying). It does two things:

  1. Packs / compacts the mortar at the joint face → better resistance to water penetration.
  2. Gives the joint its shape (profile).

Exception: an extruded joint is left squeezed out, not tooled.

Joint size

⅜" is the most common joint for CMU and modular brick (¼"–½" for non-modular brick). Your CMU lab used ⅜" joints with a concave tooled profile.

Why tooled profiles win

Concave, V and weathered joints are tooled and slope or curve so water sheds off the face. Raked and struck joints leave a ledge that holds water — poor outside.

Quick check
Give one reason mortar joints are tooled.
Tooling compacts (packs) the joint surface and shapes it — both help keep water out.
